Visualizing Hyperspectral Brain Images and Understanding Spectral Signatures

Exploring brain tissue behaviour across different wavelengths and understanding spectral signatures for image segmentation.

Research Progress Update

During the current phase of my research internship at BIT Mesra, I have been working on understanding hyperspectral brain datasets and their visualization. Before developing segmentation models, it is important to understand how information is represented within the dataset.

What Is Hyperspectral Imaging?

Unlike traditional RGB images that contain only Red, Green and Blue channels, hyperspectral images contain dozens or even hundreds of spectral bands. Each pixel stores a detailed spectral profile across different wavelengths.

This additional information allows us to study tissue characteristics that may not be visible in standard images.

Visualizing Spectral Bands

One of the first tasks was visualizing individual spectral bands from the hyperspectral image cube. As different bands were observed, changes in contrast, brightness and tissue visibility became noticeable.

Some bands highlighted structures more clearly, while others revealed subtle differences between regions of the brain.

Understanding Spectral Signatures

Every tissue interacts differently with light. When light hits a tissue, some wavelengths are absorbed while others are reflected.

The reflected intensity across wavelengths forms a spectral signature. This signature acts like a fingerprint for that tissue.

By plotting intensity against wavelength, different tissue types can produce different spectral curves.

A spectral signature can reveal information that is completely hidden in a normal RGB image.

ROI Analysis

To understand spectral behaviour more closely, Regions of Interest (ROI) can be selected from different tissue areas.

The spectral response of pixels inside these regions can then be compared across all wavelengths.

This helps identify bands that may be useful for distinguishing between different tissue types.

Key Observations

  • Different wavelengths reveal different anatomical details.
  • Tissue visibility changes significantly across spectral bands.
  • Some bands provide stronger contrast than others.
  • Spectral signatures vary between different regions.
  • Hyperspectral data contains information beyond RGB imaging.

Why This Matters For Segmentation

The ultimate objective of this research is brain image segmentation. Understanding spectral signatures is an important step because segmentation models can potentially use this information to distinguish tissue classes more accurately.

The better we understand spectral behaviour, the better we can design segmentation approaches that take advantage of hyperspectral information.

Next Steps

  • Generate spectral signature plots.
  • Compare tissue responses across wavelengths.
  • Study hyperspectral segmentation literature.
  • Prepare data for machine learning experiments.

Reflection

This stage of the project has helped me understand how hyperspectral datasets differ from conventional images. Every visualization reveals more information about the relationship between wavelength and tissue characteristics.

These observations will form the foundation for future segmentation experiments and model development.
